# Wangari — The Belief (Brand Constitution)

## 1. Why this file exists

Wangari is not a piece of software that happens to help farmers. It is a **movement** that happens to run on software. A product competes on features and loses to whoever has more money. A movement competes on belief and wins by repetition.

This file exists so that in year three, when there is pressure to discount, to rebrand, to chase an enterprise client, to rename the product, or to add a feature that offends the farmer — there is a written answer to *what are we actually for*.

We borrowed the lesson from Apple: they did not survive by out-spending anyone. They survived by owning a **belief**, naming an **enemy**, designing a **ritual**, creating **status objects**, and being a **recognizable face**. None of those five cost money. All of them cost consistency and taste. That is our entire budget.

---

## 2. The belief (say this, in these words, forever)

> **A Kenyan farmer is not a subsistence victim running on guesswork. They are a business owner who deserves to know, to the shilling, what their farm actually earns.**

This is the keynote. Every asset, every post, every field day, every partner email is some version of it. The words stay stable for years even as the features change.

**What the belief demands of us:**

| The belief | The obligation it creates |
|---|---|
| The farmer is a business owner | Numbers must be real, current, and in KES. Never rounded to look nice. |
| They deserve to *know* | If the data is missing, say so. Never fill a gap with a guess. |
| To the shilling | Costs, feed, labour, mortality — the unglamorous lines that decide whether a month was profitable. |
| Not guesswork | The offline log must work. A farmer with no network must never be punished for it. |
| Running a business | The farmer is the customer, not the data source. We never sell their records. |

**The corollary we must never break:** *one dead promise costs us an entire cooperative.* If we list WhatsApp integration, weather, Swahili, or an AI assistant, all four must work, every time. A brand is not built on marketing, it is built on the accumulated evidence that it tells the truth.

---

## 3. The enemy

**The enemy is the exercise book, the WhatsApp notes, the estimate, the "I don't remember", and the shame of not knowing.**

It is explicitly **not** other farm apps. Kenyan farmers will not compare us to an app; they will compare us to *doing nothing*. Naming the enemy correctly turns every post from an advertisement into a rally cry — and it means we never have to disparage anyone.

Say it out loud, in these terms, every week:

- *"Guesswork costs a Nakuru layer farmer KES 40,000 a year."*
- *"The pen-and-paper problem."*
- *"You already know your production. You just can't prove it."*

The pen-and-paper problem is a **brand asset**. Treat it that way.

---

## 4. The ritual (we own one moment)

Every great brand owns a signature moment. Ours already exists; it has simply never been named or repeated.

> **The 10-second log.** Airplane mode on. Three taps. Eggs counted, stored, done. Network returns, it syncs.

This is the strongest thing we will ever build and it is not a feature — it is a *demonstrable proof of belief*. The farmer who logs with no network can brag about it to anyone. It is the badge. Name it, film it, repeat it, forever.

**Format law:** the same 45-second video, the same framing, the same opening line, weekly, for years. *"Shamba yako. Dakika kumi."* Repetition is the entire strategy. A different video every week is not variety, it is amnesia.

---

## 5. Status objects (the thing we were missing)

A screenshot of "records kept" gives a farmer nothing to be proud of in front of their neighbours. **Pride is the retention engine, and it is free.** So we manufacture visible proof:

1. **The farm dashboard share card** — a designed image (farm name, this month's profit, top product, eggs/litres/kg) sized for WhatsApp Status. The farmer posts it. That is the advert we never had to pay for.
2. **The *Mkulima Wangari* certificate** — earned, not given, awarded on completing the first week of real records. Printable, screenshot-able, framed on a wall in a real house.
3. **The weekly/monthly farm report** — a single clean page a farmer can show a SACCO manager, an agrovet, or a buyer. This is also how we sell to institutions.

A farmer who can *show* what they built does not churn. Design these with care or do not ship them.

---

## 6. The face

The face is me, and the face must be consistent: same voice, same framing, Swahili-first, filmed on a real farm, in real light, never a stock photo and never a logo. In a community this small, trust runs entirely through a recognizable human. One human, repeatedly, for years. That is the job.

## 7. The name system

Original Swahili naming, not translation. A Kenyan product that *sounds* Kenyan is a moat no English competitor can copy.

| Layer | Name | Use |
|---|---|---|
| The brand | **Wangari** | Wangari Maathai — growth, roots, endurance, green. Never rename it. |
| The rally cry (Swahili) | **Shamba yako. Hesabu yako.** | "Your farm. Your numbers." — the tagline, on site, in video, in WhatsApp bio. |
| The line for institutions (English) | **From guesswork to growth.** | Partner decks, grant applications, SACCO and county conversations. |
| The signature moment | **Rekodi ya dakika kumi** | The 10-second log. |
| The badge | **Mkulima Wangari** | The farmer who knows their numbers. |
| The enemy | **Kubahatiana / guesswork** | The pen-and-paper problem. |
| The event | **Wangari Field Day** | The monthly on-farm demo. Our Apple Store. |
| The community | **Familia ya Wangari** | The WhatsApp group. Believers, not customers. |

**Swahili-first naming rule:** every module, screen, and navigation item in the farmer-facing product gets an original Swahili name. Records, Flocks, Workers, Sales, Profit — all of them. Institutions get English on request; farmers get Swahili by default.

## 8. Eight rules (the constitution)

1. **The tagline is fixed.** *Shamba yako. Hesabu yako.* Same words, everywhere, for years.
2. **The ritual is filmed weekly.** Non-negotiable. Consistency beats quality here.
3. **Name the enemy, never a competitor.** We rally, we don't compare.
4. **Promise nothing that isn't shipped.** One broken feature costs a whole cooperative.
5. **Swahili by default, English on request.** Including in marketing copy, emails, and video.
6. **Offline is sacred.** A farmer with no network is never punished. This is the proof of the belief.
7. **Never be the cheap option.** We are the upgrade, the status choice. Growth is the plan we want people on; Starter is the on-ramp. Discounting the brand is worse than losing the sale.
8. **The farmer's data is never sold.** Not to agrovets, not to co-ops, not to input companies, no matter how good the offer. This is the line we do not cross for money.

## 9. Monetisation by sponsorship, not discounting

The promo and partner system *is* the growth weapon. Every code carries an organisation's name. A group chairperson, agrovet, or cooperative manager hands out codes bearing *their* name, which makes them look good for giving free months — so they promote us, not because we paid them, but because we made them look generous.

This is the Apple-store-philosophy version of growth in a KES 0 budget: **let the people who already have the farmers' trust carry the brand for us, and make it profitable to them.** Generate sponsor-branded code batches before sending a single email. The partnership prospect list in [partnership-prospects.md](partnership-prospects.md) is the target list.

---

## 10. The honest warning (Apple's trap)

Apple outspends everyone on polish. We cannot, so we must not compete on that axis — we win on the opposite one. **Apple's weakness is that it does not know a single farmer's name. We do.** So the Wangari version must be warmer, more local, more personal, and entirely unbothered by being big. Ten farmers who feel personally seen beat ten thousand who saw an advertisement.

A brand is not built in a month of marketing, and not undone in one either. It is built by the accumulated evidence that we told the truth, every week, for years. That is the whole game.
