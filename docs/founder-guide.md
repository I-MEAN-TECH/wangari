## Part 1 — The money you need to know cold

You do not need an MBA. You need five numbers and the sentences around them. Memorise these.

### 1.1 Your pricing and what it means (from the live plans table)

| Plan | Price | Term | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter Monthly** | KES 1,500 | 30 days | Smallholder just getting records going |
| **Starter Annual** | KES 12,000 | 365 days | Same, paid yearly (~33% cheaper) |
| **Growth Monthly** | KES 4,500 | 30 days | Serious multi-enterprise/commercial |
| **Growth Annual** | KES 36,000 | 365 days | Same, yearly (~33% cheaper) |

**What a paying farmer is worth (Annual Direct Equivalent — ADE):**
- Starter: KES 1,500 × 12 ≈ **KES 18,000/year** (if retained a full year)
- Growth: KES 4,500 × 12 ≈ **KES 54,000/year**

These are your *revenue-per-paying-farmer* anchors. Every business model question reduces to: **how many paying farmers × what price = how much per year.**

### 1.2 The five numbers (know these without thinking)

1. **Revenue today: KES 0.** Be ready to say it calmly. It is not a shameful secret; it is a *when*, not an *if*.
2. **Users today: 9.** Small. Same framing.
3. **Price point: KES 1,500/month Starter** is your "does a farmer feel this is worth it" anchor. Farmers compare it to a *bag of feed* — it must feel trivially small next to a single bad decision the app prevents.
4. **Unit economics (the real ones):** Paystack takes ~1.5% per transaction (capped ~KES 70), so a Starter payment nets ~KES 1,477. Your *only* meaningful cost is a fraction of one VPS (~$20–40/month) and your own time. **Gross margin is ~99%** because software has no marginal cost per farmer. This is the single strongest financial fact you have — lead with it in any investor conversation.
5. **The path to first revenue:** 100 paying farmers × KES 1,500 = **KES 150,000/month (~$1,150/month)**. 1,000 paying farmers = KES 1.5M/month (~$11.5K/month). This is how you talk about scale without lying.

### 1.3 How to talk about "no money, no revenue" without flinching

Investors and partners have heard a thousand pitches from zero-revenue founders. What they have *not* heard a thousand times is a founder who is **calm and specific** about the gap. Practice these exact sentences:

- **"Revenue is zero today. I chose to build the engine properly first — offline, payments, security — because a farm app that breaks offline in the field is worthless. Now I go to market."**
- **"I have 9 real users and live production. I'm not hiding that. What I have is a working, paid-ready platform and a partnership-led plan to fill it."**
- **"My gross margin is ~99% because it's software. The moment a farmer pays, almost all of it is profit. That's why every partner I sign is worth a fortune relative to my costs."**

Never say: inflated user counts, "we have huge traction," fake testimonials, or a roadmap you can't back. Integrity is a compounding asset — see Part 4.

## Part 2 — The three doors: partners, grants, investors (and which to knock first)

Most first-time founders only think "investors." That's the hardest, slowest door. There are three, and you should work them in this order.

### Door 1 — Partnerships & paying customers (FASTEST, do this first)
- **Who:** co-ops, dairy co-ops, agrovet chains, SACCOs, farmer group chairpersons, national associations, county extension officers. The list is in [partnership-prospects.md](partnership-prospects.md).
- **What you want:** free access for their members + a short onboarding session + a small commission or co-branded promo code. **They have the farmers; you have the tool. Neither can do it alone.**
- **Why first:** it's the fastest path to *revenue and proof*, which is what every other door requires. A signed partner is worth more to an investor than any deck.
- **Your unfair weapon:** the **sponsor-branded promo code system you already built.** Give the chairperson a batch of codes with *their* organisation's name on them. They become your promoter because they look good handing out free months. This is KES 0 growth.

### Door 2 — Grants & funders (MEDIUM, high value if you hit the right ones)
- **Who to approach:** agricultural development NGOs, county governments, foundation grants, farm-input/feed companies with market-development budgets, incubators with modest grants, and agricultural extension programmes.
- **The two funding types:**
  - **Non-dilutive grants/innovation funds** — you keep 100% of whatever they give; no equity, no repayment. Best for you right now.
  - **Impact-linked / results-based funding** — funders pay when farmers activate on your platform. This is *perfect* for you: it converts your traction into funding.
- **What they want:** a clear social/economic impact story ("help smallholder farmers track profit and lift yield"), a working tool, and a credible person. They rarely demand revenue. Your Swahili, offline, whole-farm platform is exactly the kind of thing these funds love.
- **The move:** a crisp 2-page grant proposal (problem, solution, impact numbers, budget, team) using the vision doc as the story. [Ask me to draft it — I can write the full proposal from the audit and vision docs.]

### Door 3 — Investors (SLOWEST, but they fund the dream)
- **Who:** early-stage VCs in African agritech/fintech, pre-seed funds, accelerators (e.g. Y Combinator, Techstars-style, African accelerators), and angel investors who farm or invest in Kenya.
- **Reality check:** most seed investors will ask "show me traction." You have 9 users. **That is the gap to close before pitching equity.** The single most valuable thing you can do for your investor story is *land one partner cohort and a few hundred farmers first*, then pitch.
- **How to reach them without warm intros:** email the fund directly with the honest one-liner (Part 4), follow up once, and ask for a 15-minute call. Cold email to a fund's partner works more often than founders think, if it's honest and specific.

**The order matters: Partners → Grants → Investors.** Do not spend months chasing investors while your user base is 9. Money follows traction; chase traction first, and the money (of all three kinds) arrives faster.

## Part 3 — Financial literacy, the short and true version

You don't need to become a CFO. Learn these six ideas and you'll be dangerous in any room:

1. **Revenue vs. profit vs. cash.** Revenue is what you earn, profit is what you keep after costs, cash is what's in the account *now*. You have **none** of the three right now. Investors know this; saying it calmly is fine, panicking about it is not.
2. **Gross margin.** What you keep per shilling of revenue, before fixed costs. Your margin is ~99% (software). Very high margin = very valuable business. Say this number out loud in a pitch.
3. **Unit economics (LTV vs. CAC).** LTV = how much a customer pays you over their lifetime. CAC = what it cost to win them. Right now your CAC is ~KES 0 (founder's own time) and your LTV, if a farmer stays a year on Starter, is ~KES 18,000. That's a phenomenal ratio — *once you have paying farmers*, you can show an investor a very good LTV:CAC story. (Honesty note: right now you have no paying farmers, so this is a *potential* story; get the first 10 paying farmers and it becomes a *real* one.)
4. **Burn and runway.** Burn = monthly loss (money *and* time). Runway = how long you last. You burn almost no cash — your runway is "until this stops being sustainable on effort alone." The honest risk isn't money, it's **founder burnout and key-person risk**.
5. **Burn multiple / efficiency.** How much cash you burn per KES 1 of revenue. Right now: infinite (revenue 0). The goal is to get this to < 1 as revenue starts. This is the number sophisticated investors watch.
6. **Equity basics (if an investor comes):** they buy a *percentage*, not a salary. A pre-seed round of, say, KES 10M for 20% means they put in 10M and expect the company to be worth ~50M later. **Do not give away equity casually or early** — a little goes a long way, and once given it can't be un-given. Get a lawyer before signing anything.

## Part 4 — What to actually SAY (the pitch, in four lengths)

Rehearse these until they're boring to you. Adapt, never memorise verbatim.

### The 10-second version (elevator / handshake / passing someone at an event)
> *"I'm building Wangari — a farm management app for Kenyan smallholders. It tracks eggs, milk, feed, workers and sales, works without internet, and is in Swahili and KES. Right now it has 9 users and no revenue — I'm going after farm co-operatives to drive it. Do you know any farmers or co-ops who'd want this?"*

*(Notice: it ends in a question, which turns a pitch into a conversation — and an event is for conversations, not pitches.)*

### The 60-second version (most of your pitches — the one to master)
> *"Most Kenyan farmers run their farm from memory and a notebook — they don't know what they actually earn until the month is over, and by then it's too late to fix. I built **Wangari**: a whole-farm app that records production, feed, labour and sales, works fully offline, and shows real profit in KES. It covers poultry, dairy, goats, pigs, fish and crops — not just one thing. It's in Swahili, takes M-Pesa payments, and integrates WhatsApp. It's live and secure; I hard-cut the security myself. Today it has 9 real users and zero revenue — I'm honest about that. What I have is a working, defensible product and a partnership plan: I give each cooperative free accounts for their members and a personal onboarding session, which is how I'll reach thousands of farmers. I have 13 verified partners prospects identified and I'm sending outreach this week. I'm looking for [partners / grant funding / a first paying cohort]."*

### The 3-minute version (a real meeting with a funder, co-op, or investor)
Structure it as: **Problem → Solution → Why me/why now → Traction (honest) → The ask → The vision.**
- **Problem:** farmers decide on guesswork; a bad month (feed cost, mortality, a dropped price) is discovered too late; record-keeping is paper or nothing.
- **Solution:** Wangari — offline, Swahili, whole-farm, M-Pesa. Log in 10 seconds, know your real profit in KES.
- **Why now:** Kenya is going digital fast; whole-farm tools don't exist locally; government is pushing livestock traceability; a farmer's phone is now their bank.
- **Traction (say the real number):** live in production, 9 users, 8 farms, full payments and security, ~99% gross margin, partnership pipeline starting. **Never inflate.**
- **The ask:** be specific. *"I'm looking for one cooperative of ~200 farmers as a launch partner, and introductions to [agricultural funders / agribusiness leads]."* A specific, modest ask is 10× more likely to be granted than "I'm looking for funding."
- **The vision:** V1 records → V2 AI/automation → V3 IoT + connecting M-Pesa, sensors, and other software so Wangari is the farmer's single front door. (Use the one-liner: *V1 remembers the farm, V2 runs the farm, V3 connects the farm to everything.*)

### The 10-minute version (a pitch meeting / demo day)
Add a **live demo** (this is your biggest advantage — you're the founder, so you can do it live on your phone, on a real farm, even in Swahili). Show: logging eggs offline → turning on data → the profit number. Then show the dashboard, the partner promo code, and the M-Pesa flow. End with the ask and the vision. Have a one-page leave-behind (the vision doc makes a good backbone).

## Part 5 — Doing the room (events, meetings, field days)

Events are where partnerships are made, not where you pitch. Adjust your posture:

- **Your goal at an event is not "close funding." It is "meet three people who matter, and leave with one follow-up call booked."** Write that goal down before you walk in.
- **Listen more than you pitch.** Ask: "What do you wish you could see on your farm that you can't see today?" Their answer *is* your product roadmap and your pitch, in their words.
- **Bring the demo, not the slides.** A phone in your hand logging eggs offline is worth more than 30 slides. If there's WiFi, great; if not, that's the point — *it works without WiFi.*
- **Be ready for these three hard questions** (they will come; practice calm honesty):
  - *"How many users do you have?"* → "Nine, and zero revenue. I'm building the engine properly first; now I'm scaling through partners."
  - *"How will you make money?"* → "Subscriptions — KES 1,500/month Starter. Software margin is ~99%. The first 100 paying farmers is KES 150,000/month, and it compounds from there."
  - *"What's your competition?"* → "Most farm tools here are poultry-only or paper. I cover the whole farm — livestock *and* crops — offline, in Swahili, with M-Pesa. That's the gap."
- **Always ask how THEY get paid / who they serve.** Understanding their incentive tells you exactly what offer will land (a co-op wants retention; an agrovet wants sell-through; a funder wants impact numbers).
- **Collect follow-ups ruthlessly.** Every conversation ends with: "Send me the two-page doc and let's do a 15-minute call next week." Then actually send it the same day.
- **Swahili matters.** If the person is a farmer, a chairperson, or a county officer, be ready to say the demo and the value in Swahili. It is a superpower most investors don't have.

### The leave-behind stack (have these ready, printed or on your phone/laptop)
1. **One-pager** — problem, solution, screenshots, the ask.
2. **The demo** (a 60–90s video of the offline log, pre-recorded, for people you can't demo to live).
3. **Vision doc** (the story) and **valuation audit** (the honesty — share the audit *proactively* with serious investors; it builds enormous trust).

## Part 7 — Founder rules (keep these; they outlast any single pitch)

1. **Never invent a number.** Your integrity is the one asset you cannot rebuild. Nine users said honestly beats a hundred users lied about.
2. **Ask specifically.** "I'd like to introduce you to 200 farmers" and "I need 15 minutes of your time" are grantable; "I'm looking for funding" is not.
3. **Partners before investors.** Traction is the thing investors buy. Get traction first.
4. **Write down every conversation** (name, what they said, the follow-up, the date). Follow up once, then move on. Your memory is not reliable at this volume.
5. **Protect the belief.** Never oversell a feature that isn't shipped ([vision.md](vision.md) rule 4). A broken promise from a founder is the fastest way to lose a community forever.
6. **Get a lawyer before giving away equity or signing anything.** Non-negotiable. Your time is better spent on the product; equity is the one thing you must get right.
