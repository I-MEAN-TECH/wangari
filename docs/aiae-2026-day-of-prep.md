## 4. The pitch, in three lengths

### 10-second (handshake)
> "I'm Lewis, I build Wangari — a farm app for Kenyan smallholders. It tracks eggs, milk, feed, workers, sales; works fully offline; in Swahili and KES. Thirteen users, no revenue yet — I'm scaling through co-ops and feed partners. Do you know any cooperatives or feed dealers who'd want this?"

### 60-second (the workhorse)
> "Most Kenyan farmers run their farm from memory and a notebook. They find out what they earned when the month is over — too late to fix it. Wangari records production, feed, labour and sales; works fully offline; and shows real profit in KES. It covers the whole farm — poultry, dairy, goats, pigs, fish and crops — not just one enterprise. It takes M-Pesa payments and speaks Swahili. Today: 13 real users, zero revenue — I'm honest about that. What I have is a working, defensible product and a partnership plan: free accounts for a cooperative's or feed dealer's farmers, plus a personal onboarding session. I'm here to meet a few [feed companies / cooperatives / counties] to run that with. Who's the biggest farmer group you reach?"

### 3-minute (a real meeting)
**Structure:** Problem → Solution → Why now → Traction (honest) → The ask → The vision.
- **Problem:** farmers decide on guesswork; a bad month is discovered too late; records are paper or nothing.
- **Solution:** Wangari — offline, Swahili, whole-farm, M-Pesa. Real profit in KES.
- **Why now:** Kenya is going digital fast; whole-farm tools don't exist locally; government is pushing livestock traceability.
- **Traction:** live in production, 13 users, full payments and security, ~99% gross margin, partnership pipeline starting. **Never inflate.**
- **The ask:** specific — "one cooperative of ~200 farmers as a launch partner" beats "I'm looking for funding."
- **The vision:** *V1 remembers the farm. V2 runs it. V3 connects it to everything.*

### The three hard questions
- *"How many users?"* → "Thirteen, zero revenue — I built the engine properly first, now I'm scaling through partners."
- *"How will you make money?"* → "Subscriptions, KES 1,500/month Starter. ~99% software margin. 100 paying farmers is KES 150,000/month, and it compounds."
- *"Who's your competition?"* → "Most tools here are poultry-only or paper. I cover the whole farm — livestock and crops — offline, Swahili, M-Pesa. That's the gap."

### The one specific ask (pick ONE per conversation)
- **Feed company:** "Give your dealer network free Wangari accounts and I'll onboard the farmers; you get stickier farmers and real feed-usage data. Pilot with 100 of your farmers?"
- **County:** "Let us onboard 100 farmers in your county and show the results publicly."
- **Co-op:** "Free accounts plus a training day for your members; I'll come run it. Let's start with one group."
- **Investor:** "I'm not raising a priced round yet — I have 13 users. Give me 90 days to land one co-op cohort and I'll come back with traction. For now, an intro to [fund/partner] is what I need."

## 6. If a pilot conversation gets warm

Pull out the **Pilot LOI** (`pilot-loi-template.md`) and fill it in together on the spot:
- Number of farmers to onboard
- Pilot start date and duration (suggest 60–90 days)
- Enterprises covered (poultry/dairy/crops/etc.)
- What success looks like — be specific, together
- Get both signatures before the conversation ends

A signed LOI is worth more than any single investor conversation — it's the asset the whole trip is really for.

## 7. If an investor asks about terms

Reference from memory (`convertible-note-term-sheet.md`) — don't hand out paper:
- Instrument: a Kenyan **convertible loan agreement** (the local equivalent of a SAFE)
- Amount: KES 500,000 – 3,000,000, indicative
- Valuation cap: KES 15–25M, indicative, anchored to cost-to-rebuild
- Discount: 15–20% to the next priced round
- No board seat or priced round at this size — keep it simple
- Always add: "have a Kenyan lawyer review before anything is signed."

## 8. Rules for every conversation

1. **Go to meet, not to sell.** Goal: 3 real conversations, 1 booked follow-up.
2. **Never inflate.** 13 users, 0 revenue — said calmly, every time.
3. **Collect before you pitch.** First question to a new person: "What's the biggest farmer pain point you're seeing?" Their answer becomes your pitch.
4. **Name the enemy, not competitors:** guesswork and the notebook — not other apps.
5. **Swahili for farmers/county/extension; English for investors/institutions.** Be ready in both.
6. **Write everything down** — name, org, what they said, the follow-up owed, the date. Follow up within 24 hours.
7. **Same energy, same story, every day.** Consistency is the brand.

## 9. End of each day

- [ ] Before leaving the venue, write down every name/org/follow-up while it's fresh.
- [ ] Send the "here's what I promised" email **that same night**, not the next day.
- [ ] Note which conversations are warm enough for a signed LOI or a scheduled call.

## 10. What a good outcome looks like

- **Good (likely, if prepared):** 3–8 real conversations, 1–2 warm partnership leads, 1–2 investor/funder conversations, a pile of named follow-ups.
- **Great (if a pitch slot or MOU signing happens):** a headline moment that materially moves Wangari's credibility.
- **What actually matters:** not the contact count — the **one relationship that becomes a real pilot.** Everything else is a step toward that.
