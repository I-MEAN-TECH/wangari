## 6. The pitch (adapt from founder-guide; the expo-specific version)

**10-second (handshake):**
> "I'm Lewis, I build Wangari — a farm app for Kenyan smallholders. It tracks eggs, milk, feed, workers, sales; works fully offline; in Swahili and KES. Nine users, no revenue yet — I'm scaling through co-ops and feed partners. Do you know any cooperatives or feed dealers who'd want this for their farmers?"

**60-second (the workhorse):**
> "Most Kenyan farmers run their farm from memory and a notebook. They find out what they earned when the month is over — too late to fix it. **Wangari** records production, feed, labour and sales; it works **fully offline**; and it shows your real profit in KES. It covers the **whole farm** — poultry, dairy, goats, pigs, fish *and* crops — not just one enterprise. It takes **M-Pesa** payments, speaks Swahili, and integrates WhatsApp. It's live and secure. **Today: 9 real users, zero revenue** — I'm honest about that. What I have is a working, defensible product and a partnership plan: I give each cooperative and feed dealer free accounts for their farmers plus a personal onboarding session. I'm here at AIAE to meet a few [feed companies / cooperatives / counties] to run that with. Who's the biggest farmer group you reach?"

**The 3 hard questions, memorised:**
- "How many users?" → "Nine, and no revenue yet — I'm building properly first, then scaling through partners."
- "How will you make money?" → "Subscriptions, KES 1,500/month Starter. Software margin is ~99%. 100 paying farmers = KES 150,000/month, and it compounds."
- "Who's your competition?" → "Most tools here are poultry-only or paper. I cover the whole farm — livestock *and* crops — offline, Swahili, M-Pesa. That's the gap."

**The one specific ask (pick ONE per conversation):**
- To a **feed company**: "Give your dealer network free Wangari accounts and I'll onboard the farmers; you get stickier farmers and real feed-usage data. Pilot with 100 of your farmers?"
- To a **county**: "Let us onboard 100 farmers in your county and show the results publicly — digital smallholder adoption you can point to."
- To a **co-op**: "Free accounts + a training day for your members; I'll come run it. Let's start with one group."
- To an **investor**: "I'm not raising yet — I have 9 users. Give me 90 days to land one co-op cohort and I'll come back with traction. For now, an intro to [fund/partner] is what I need."

## 7. The rules for the expo (integrity + focus)

1. **Go to meet, not to sell.** Your goal: "meet three people who matter, leave with one booked follow-up." Write that on a card in your pocket.
2. **Never inflate.** 9 users, 0 revenue. Say it calmly. Every person you meet can open your app in 5 minutes and see it's real and good. The honesty *is* the pitch.
3. **Listen first.** "What's the biggest farmer pain point you see?" — their words become your pitch and your roadmap.
4. **Collect, don't pitch, in the first 60 seconds** of a new conversation; then ask a question; then pitch if there's interest.
5. **Name the enemy, not competitors** (see [vision.md](vision.md)): the enemy is guesswork and the notebook, not other apps.
6. **Swahili for farmers/county/extension; English for investors/institutions.** Be ready in both.
7. **Write everything down** (name, org, what they said, the follow-up, the date). Follow up within 24 hours, then once more, then let it go.
8. **You are the face.** Same energy, same story, three days running. Consistency is the brand.
